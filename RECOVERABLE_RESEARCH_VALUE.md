## Six surviving research directions

### 1. Iontronic state-machine model

The strongest surviving abstraction is not "water remembers." It is:

[
u(t) longrightarrow x(t) longrightarrow y(t)
]

where:
- (u(t)) = controlled electrical/chemical stimulus;
- (x(t)) = internal ionic/polarization/material state;
- (y(t)) = measurable electrical readout.

A memory candidate exists only if (x(t)) and/or (y(t)) depend reproducibly on prior input history under controlled conditions.

### 5. Information loss and recoverability

The P100 distinction between state and projection applies directly.

[
x_{	ext{internal}}ightarrow y_{	ext{electrical}}
]

may be many-to-one. Different ionic configurations can potentially yield indistinguishable measured outputs.

Therefore any future governed descendant should record:
- what state is directly observed;
- what is inferred;
- whether the inverse is unique;
- what auxiliary measurements/conditions are required;
- what information is irrecoverably lost.

**Rule:** many-to-one physical projection requires an explicit information-loss/recoverability witness.

### 6. Mechanism-specific decomposition

"Water" should not be treated as a single causal mechanism. Candidate mechanisms must be separated where applicable:

- ionic migration/diffusion;
- electric double-layer polarization;
- electrode/interface effects;
- channel geometry;
- electrolyte concentration/composition;
- temperature;
- surface charge/chemistry;
- electrochemical reactions;
- phase/material changes.

This prevents a device-level hysteresis observation from being incorrectly attributed to molecular water alone.

## What did not survive

The following do not belong in the reconstructed core without new independent evidence:

- ordinary bulk water as persistent symbolic memory;
- holographic water archive;
- biological memory transfer/storage attributed generically to water;
- consciousness or identity implications;
- medical/diagnostic efficacy;
- implant safety/efficacy;
- specific pW, ns, µs, days, capacity or market-performance claims from the historical README;
- the claim that molecular dipole orientation alone constitutes a stable writable memory bit;
- historical `RSCS_water` as canonical RSCS.

Preservation of these statements in the legacy source is historical provenance, not scientific endorsement.
