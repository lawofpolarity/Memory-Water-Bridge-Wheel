## 2026-09-27 literature verification pass

A current literature check confirms that the **bounded iontronic-device direction is real prior art**, while it does not validate the historical generic-water claims.

- Kamsma et al., *Brain-inspired computing with fluidic iontronic nanochannels*, PNAS 121, e2320242121 (2024), is a real publication. Current review literature identifies it as nanofluidic/iontronic memristor work.
- Pandey et al., *Nanofluidic ionic memory for next-generation computing*, Nature Reviews Materials (2026), reviews history-dependent conductance arising from ion transport under nanoscale confinement and emphasizes surface chemistry, geometry, electrolyte properties, mechanism identification, variability and integration challenges.
- Perez-Carvajal et al., *Macroscale nanofluidic memristors enable iontronic memory, sensing and programmable separation*, Communications Materials (2026), reports experimentally confirmed memristive response from asymmetric ion-polarization dynamics in nanochannels.
- Recent fluidic/gel iontronic work also reports device-specific hysteresis, write/read decay and neuromorphic functions.

**Interpretation:** these sources strengthen the reconstruction choice to study a specified iontronic device. They do not establish that ordinary bulk water stores symbolic, holographic or biological memories, and they do not validate the MWB equations by association.

The historical 0.7 µW figure, specific digit-error description, market figures and “Fluidic Memristor Toolkit” remain unverified here and should not be reused without exact source matching.
